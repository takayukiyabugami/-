# Revision Rubric

## Diagnosis Categories

Use these categories when a draft feels flat, confusing, over-written, or emotionally weak.

| Category | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Desire | What does the POV character want in this scene? |
| Pressure | What gets worse if they fail or wait? |
| Opposition | Who or what pushes back? |
| Turn | What changes from beginning to end? |
| Cost | What does the choice damage, reveal, or risk? |
| Specificity | Which nouns, verbs, and images feel generic? |
| Rhythm | Where does the pace sag or rush? |
| Subtext | What is being said too directly? |
| Continuity | What contradicts established facts or tone? |
| Aftertaste | What remains in the reader after the scene ends? |
| Reader action | What does the passage make the reader want to do next? |

## Revision Priorities

1. Fix scene purpose before sentence polish.
2. Strengthen desire and opposition before adding lore.
3. Cut explanation that characters would not think in the moment.
4. Replace generic emotion with pressure-specific behavior.
5. Keep one strong image instead of three weak images.
6. Make the final beat carry consequence.
7. For desire-driven prose, replace claims with sensory sequence and action trigger.

## Rewrite Modes

- **Light polish**: preserve structure and meaning; improve rhythm and image.
- **Scene repair**: keep canon but rebuild pressure, order, and emotional turn.
- **Deep rewrite**: change structure, conflict, or ending with explicit rationale.
- **Editorial memo**: no prose rewrite; return diagnosis and next revision tasks.
- **Desire pass**: preserve subject; increase appetite, urgency, sensory proof, and reader action.

## Response Template

```markdown
## Diagnosis
- ...

## Revision Direction
- ...

## Rewritten Draft
...

## Notes
- ...
```

Do not write "it is good" without evidence. If a passage works, name why it works and protect that element during revision.
