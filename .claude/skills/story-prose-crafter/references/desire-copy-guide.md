# Desire Copy Guide

## Purpose

Use this guide when the user wants prose that makes someone want to eat, buy, visit, try, keep reading, or act. The goal is not explanation. The goal is appetite.

## Core Pattern

1. Start at the point of contact: heat, scent, sound, color, weight, surface, first touch.
2. Delay the reward for one or two beats.
3. Give the reader a physical next action: pick it up, lean closer, break it open, take a bite, step inside, press play.
4. Deliver the payoff with texture and consequence.
5. End with a decision-shaped line.

## Food Writing

- Use temperature before taste.
- Use texture before abstract praise.
- Make smell travel through space.
- Let sound imply freshness: crackle, hiss, tap, tear, pour.
- Show danger when useful: too hot, melting, dripping, staining, disappearing fast.
- Mention sharing only if it increases urgency or warmth.

## Product or Place Writing

- Show what changes when the reader uses it or enters it.
- Make benefits concrete through scene, not slogans.
- Avoid feature lists unless the user asks for marketing copy.
- Put the strongest sensory or practical payoff near the end.

## Weak Moves To Avoid

- "It is delicious", "最高", "魅力的", "心躍る" without proof.
- Too many adjectives before the object appears.
- Metaphors that hide the actual experience.
- A nostalgic mood with no present-tense action.
- Ending with a vague invitation like "ぜひ味わってください" unless requested.

## Fast Checklist

- Can the reader see the object in the first two sentences?
- Is there a clear sequence from noticing to wanting?
- Is at least one texture specific?
- Is the final line an action trigger?
