# Prose Style Guide

## Core Standard

Make prose emotionally legible and structurally useful. A sentence should reveal pressure, character, setting, or consequence. If it only sounds fine, cut or compress it.

## Scene Pressure

- Open near a disturbance, decision, secret, or unmet desire.
- Give the POV character something specific to protect, gain, hide, or avoid.
- Let each exchange change status, distance, information, or risk.
- End on a consequence, not a decorative fade-out.

## Emotional Writing

- Show emotion through selection: what the character notices, ignores, misreads, or cannot name.
- Use body sensation sparingly and specifically.
- Avoid direct labels like "sad", "angry", or "lonely" when an action can carry it.
- Let contradiction create depth: wanting comfort while refusing help, seeking truth while lying, protecting someone while resenting them.

## Rhythm

- Use shorter paragraphs when pressure rises.
- Use longer sentences for drift, memory, temptation, or sensory immersion.
- Put the sharpest word near the end of a paragraph when possible.
- Vary dialogue beats. Do not attach a gesture to every line.

## Imagery

- Prefer images tied to the character's history, fear, trade, or desire.
- Repeat one charged image with variation to create motif.
- Avoid stacked metaphors in the same paragraph.
- Do not use atmosphere as a substitute for conflict.

## Dialogue

- Give every line an agenda: obtain, deflect, test, wound, comfort, conceal, force.
- Let subtext carry what the character cannot say directly.
- Use silence only when it changes the balance of power.
- Keep speech patterns distinct but not gimmicky.

## Chapter Endings

Use one of these:

- new information that redefines the scene
- irreversible choice
- emotional reversal
- immediate threat
- promise made under pressure
- quiet line that exposes the cost
